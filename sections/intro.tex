\section{Introduction}

An ordinary Riordan array is determined by two formal power series
$(g(t),f(t))$, and its $k$th column has generating function
\[
H_k(t)=g(t)f(t)^k.
\]
Krylov used circulant matrices to describe the periodic blocks for a family
with polynomial second component \cite{Krylov2025}. Bellamy, Pflomm, and
Krylov subsequently developed periodicity results for polynomial Riordan
arrays over finite fields, including higher-dimensional arrays and periodic
column partial sums \cite{BellamyPflommKrylov2026}. The present paper replaces
the polynomial second component by a rational one. Its denominators then vary
with the column index, so the block length is no longer fixed.

When $g$ and $f$ are rational over a finite field, every column sequence is
eventually periodic, as follows from the standard theory of rational
generating functions over finite fields \cite{Golomb1967,LidlNiederreiter1997}.
The denominator of $H_k$ changes with $k$ when $f$ is rational but not
polynomial. The resulting growth in factor multiplicities causes the periods
of neighboring columns to change in a restricted way.

Write
\[
g(t)=\frac{p_1(t)}{p_2(t)},
\qquad
f(t)=\frac{p_3(t)}{p_4(t)}
\]
over a finite field $\mathbb F_q$ of characteristic $p$, and assume that
$p_2$ and $p_4$ are nonzero with nonzero constant terms. We also impose the
usual Riordan conditions $g(0)\ne0$, $f(0)=0$, and $f'(0)\ne0$. Before cancellation,
the denominator of column $k$ is
\[
Q_k(t)=p_2(t)p_4(t)^k.
\]
Let $\pi_k$ be the least positive integer $N$ for which $Q_k$ divides
$t^N-1$. If the numerator and denominator of $H_k$ are coprime, then $\pi_k$
is the least eventual period of column $k$. Without that coprimality
hypothesis, it is still an eventual period, but it need not be least.

Our first main conclusion is that $(\pi_k)$ forms a tower controlled by the
characteristic.

\begin{letterthm}\label{thm:intro-period}
Suppose $k\geqslant1$. Then
\[
\pi_{k+1}=\pi_k
\qquad\text{or}\qquad
\pi_{k+1}=p\pi_k.
\]
Consequently, there is a nondecreasing sequence $(s_k)_{k\geqslant1}$ such
that
\[
\pi_k=\pi_1p^{s_k}.
\]
\end{letterthm}

This behavior follows from the Frobenius identity
\[
(t^N-1)^p=t^{pN}-1.
\]
It also has an explicit factorization description. If
\[
Q_k=\prod_i\varphi_i^{a_i+kb_i},
\]
then
\[
\pi_k=E p^{u_k},
\]
where $E$ is the least common multiple of the orders of the distinct
irreducible factors $\varphi_i$, and $u_k$ is the largest base-$p$ ceiling
logarithm of their multiplicities.

The second conclusion concerns the periodic blocks themselves. An
$N$-periodic block is naturally an element of the circulant ring
\[
\mathcal C_N=\mathbb F_q[t]/(t^N-1),
\]
where multiplication by $t$ acts as cyclic shift. We use the standard
polynomial model of circulant matrices \cite{Davis1979}. The identity
\[
p_4(t)H_{k+1}(t)=p_3(t)H_k(t)
\]
therefore descends to an equation between sufficiently late periodic blocks.

\begin{letterthm}\label{thm:intro-block}
Let $N=\pi_{k+1}$, and read length-$N$ blocks $B_k$ and $B_{k+1}$ from the
same sufficiently late position in columns $k$ and $k+1$. Then
\[
p_4(t)B_{k+1}=p_3(t)B_k
\qquad\text{in }\mathcal C_N.
\]
The block $B_k$ is obtained by repeating a length-$\pi_k$ block
$N/\pi_k$ times.
\end{letterthm}

The coefficient $p_4(t)$ is generally not invertible in $\mathcal C_N$.
Indeed, if $p_4$ is nonconstant, then $p_4$ divides $t^N-1$. The block
relation is therefore a linear correspondence, not a uniquely invertible
transition from one column to the next.

Section~2 presents the case of $\mathbb F_5$ in the notation and scope of an
accompanying Lean formalization.\footnote{The Lean source and project files
are available at
\url{https://github.com/JonBannon/RiordanArrays/tree/master/RiordanFormalization}.}
The formal development includes polynomial
orders, least eventual periods, the period-ratio theorem, the circulant block
relation, the repetition map, and the Pascal example. Section~3 gives the
corresponding statements over an arbitrary finite field $\mathbb F_q$ of
characteristic $p$. This separation records exactly which specialization has
been formally checked while retaining the natural finite-field formulation.
